# Bölüm 3: Timur'un Semerkant'ı, son seferi ve ölümü, Gür-i Emir, 1941 mezar açılışı ve mirası

> Labels: **VERIFIED** = confirmed by a reliable source (Iranica, Britannica, academic, or several independent sources agreeing). **DISPUTED** = sources conflict, or only weak sources support it. **LEGEND** = folklore or a story with no documentary support.
> Method note: the network blocked direct page fetches (Wikipedia, Britannica, History Today and travel sites all failed DNS lookup). These findings come from search-engine extracts of the cited pages, not full-page reads. Exact quotations should be checked against the originals before they go on screen.
> Turkish spellings: Semerkant, Keş / Şehrisebz, Otrar, Gür-i Emir, Uluğ Bey, Şahruh, Bibi Hanım, Ak Saray, Babür, Halil Sultan, Pir Muhammed, Muhammed Sultan, Nadir Şah, Gerasimov, Malik Kayumov.

## 1. Samarkand (Semerkant) as capital: deported craftsmen, buildings, gardens, villages named after cities

### Takeaway
Timur filled Semerkant with architects and craftsmen deported from the cities he conquered, and he founded settlements around it named after the great Islamic cities: Mısır (Cairo), Dımaşk (Damascus), Bağdat, Sultaniye, Şiraz. Iranica confirms this, and two of the names (Şiraz, Dımaşk) still survive as village names. The Ak Saray inscription "Gücümüzden şüphe eden, binalarımıza baksın" is real in substance, but translations vary.

### Cited Findings
- **VERIFIED:** Timur deported architects, artists and craftsmen to Samarqand and founded new settlements there, "frequently named for other major Islamic cities: Cairo (Meṣr), Damascus, Baghdad, Solṭānīya, Shiraz." — [Encyclopaedia Iranica, "Courts and Courtiers v"](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/courts-and-courtiers-v/)
- **VERIFIED:** Some villages near Samarqand still carry the names of the places their people came from (Širāz, Demašq). — [Encyclopaedia Iranica, "Samarqand i. History and Archaeology"](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/samarqand-i/)
  - Note: a travel site lists only Baghdad and Shiraz ([Silk Road Adventures](https://silkadv.com/en/content/origins-samarkand)). Use the Iranica list.
- **VERIFIED:** After the sack of Damascus (1400–1401), skilled craftsmen and learned men were deported to Semerkant. The chronicler Ibn Arabşah was one of them, carried off from Damascus as a boy, and he wrote about it himself. — [Smarthistory, "Timur's entry into Samarkand, Zafarnama"](https://smarthistory.org/timur-entry-samarkand-zafarnama/); [History Today, "The Sack of Damascus"](https://www.historytoday.com/archive/months-past/sack-damascus)
- **VERIFIED (wording varies):** **Ak Saray** in Keş/Şehrisebz, Timur's home town, was built between 1380 and 1404. The portal carried the inscription "If you doubt (or challenge, or question) our power, look at our buildings," meant to impress foreign visitors. It has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2000 (Şehrisebz historic centre). — [Wikipedia, Ak-Saray Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ak-Saray_Palace); [Jahongir Travel](https://jahongir-travel.uz/blog/ak-saray-palace-shahrisabz)
- **VERIFIED:** Only the portal's pylons survive, about 38 m high; the portal vault spanned 22 m. Estimates of the original height (about 65–71 m) are **DISPUTED**. — [Wikipedia, Ak-Saray Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ak-Saray_Palace)
- **DISPUTED:** The palace was destroyed in the 16th century by Abdullah Khan II (Wikipedia); another source says Ubaydullah Khan in 1570. — [Wikipedia, Ak-Saray Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ak-Saray_Palace); [People Travels](https://people-travels.com/sights/ak-saray)
- **VERIFIED (dates vary slightly):** The **Bibi Hanım** Mosque was named after Timur's wife (Saray Mülk Hanım). One source gives 1399–1405 for its construction. — [search extract, Gur-e Amir / Timurid architecture results](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_architecture); [Wikipedia, Saray Mulk Khanum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saray_Mulk_Khanum)
- **VERIFIED (Clavijo):** Clavijo saw the frenzied pace of construction in Semerkant, goods arriving from China, and a "plain covered with gardens, and houses, and markets." — [Origins (Ohio State Univ.), "A Castilian in Samarkand, 1404"](https://origins.osu.edu/milestones/september-2012-castilian-samarkand-1404)

### Inferences
- A strong opening scene: "Bağdat, Şam, Kahire, Şiraz… hepsi Semerkant'ın köyleriydi." Timur made his capital the "centre of the world" by turning the cities he conquered into its suburbs. This is backed by Iranica.
- The Ak Saray inscription works as a narration hook. Present it as "kapısında şöyle yazıyordu" and add "rivayete göre / çevirisi" so we don't commit to exact wording.

### Gaps
- No source found for the popular claim that Indian elephants were used to build Bibi Hanım (after the 1398–99 India campaign) or for the love legend about the architect. Treat both as LEGEND unless they are sourced.
- Şah-ı Zinde and details of Timur's gardens (Bağ-ı Dilküşa, Bağ-ı Çinar, etc.) were not covered. Clavijo describes several gardens, but the exact names could not be confirmed.

## 2. Ruy González de Clavijo's embassy (1403–1406)

### Takeaway
The Castilian envoy was in Semerkant from 8 September to about 20–21 November 1404. He left the most detailed Western eyewitness account of Timur's court, describing feasts in the gardens, a celebration for the chief wife at the Kan-i Gil meadow, and an aged Timur with failing eyes and drooping eyelids. He left days before Timur set out for China.

### Cited Findings
- **VERIFIED:** The Castilians reached Semerkant on 8 September 1404 and left on 21 November, six days before Timur began his Chinese campaign. — [Origins (OSU), "A Castilian in Samarkand, 1404"](https://origins.osu.edu/milestones/september-2012-castilian-samarkand-1404)
  - Wikipedia gives 8 September to 20 November 1404 (a one-day difference). — [Wikipedia, Ruy González de Clavijo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruy_Gonz%C3%A1lez_de_Clavijo)
- **VERIFIED:** Clavijo described the elaborate court protocol for approaching Timur in his gardens and the lavish garden banquets that followed, including a feast for the chief wife on the Khan-i-Gil meadow outside the city. — [Origins (OSU)](https://origins.osu.edu/milestones/september-2012-castilian-samarkand-1404); [Wikipedia, Saray Mulk Khanum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saray_Mulk_Khanum)
- **VERIFIED (secondary quote):** Clavijo describes Timur as an old man with poor eyesight, "so old that his eyelids have fallen down completely." P. Froggatt (Medical History, Cambridge) cites Markham's 1859 Hakluyt Society translation and reads this as bilateral ptosis (drooping eyelids). He notes Timur was not fully blind: within months he was commanding in the field again and still played chess. — [P. Froggatt, "The Albinism of Timur, Zal, and Edward the Confessor", Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/64CE96101496F310C5476DFBD7F3251A/S0025727300027666a.pdf/the-albinism-of-timur-zal-and-edward-the-confessor.pdf)
- **VERIFIED:** Because of Timur's ill health, the embassy could not get a reply letter for King Henry III before it left. — [Origins (OSU)](https://origins.osu.edu/milestones/september-2012-castilian-samarkand-1404)
- **DISPUTED (age):** "He was nearly 70" comes from a podcast script, not a primary source. Timur's birth is usually given as 1336, which would make him about 68. — [Human Circus podcast](https://humancircuspodcast.com/podcastscripts/2021/1/23/ruy-gonzalez-de-clavijo-5-a-season-of-feasting-at-samarkand)

### Inferences
- A strong scene: a half-blind old conqueror at a feast, holding his eyelids up to look at the envoys, who sets out to conquer China months later. The "eyelids" detail is backed by an academic source.

### Gaps
- Clavijo's original wording (feasts with wine, whole roasted horses, tents) could not be quoted from the primary text. Check Markham 1859 or Le Strange 1928 directly.
- No direct source was found tying a 1404 "kurultay" to the wedding celebrations of the grandsons. Clavijo's feasts fall in that same autumn 1404 window, but the term and the details should be checked (Marozzi ch. on Samarkand 1404).

## 3. China (Ming, Yongle) campaign, death at Otrar, succession

### Takeaway
Timur left Semerkant in winter 1404 (late November / December) with an army often given as 200,000 to attack Ming China. He fell ill with fever at Otrar during a severe winter and died there in February 1405; the date varies between 17, 18 and 19 February depending on the source. His chosen heir Pir Muhammed was pushed aside: Halil Sultan seized Semerkant and the treasury, and Şahruh won the succession war in 1409.

### Cited Findings
- **VERIFIED:** Timur set out about 27 November 1404, six days after Clavijo's departure on 21 November. — [Origins (OSU)](https://origins.osu.edu/milestones/september-2012-castilian-samarkand-1404)
- **DISPUTED (number):** History Today says Timur marched from Semerkant with an army "200,000 strong", aiming at China, and stopped at Otrar in a severe winter. Treat the figure as conventional, not documented precisely. — [History Today, "The Death of Tamerlane"](https://www.historytoday.com/archive/months-past/death-tamerlane)
- **DISPUTED (exact day):** History Today gives 18 February 1405; Britannica gives 19 February 1405 at Otrar, near Chimkent (now Shymkent, Kazakhstan); the 1911 Britannica gives 17 February 1405. — [History Today](https://www.historytoday.com/archive/months-past/death-tamerlane); [Britannica, Timur](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Timur); [1911 Britannica on Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Tim%C5%ABr)
  - Recommendation: say "Şubat 1405" in narration, or "18 Şubat 1405" with a note that the date varies.
- **VERIFIED:** He fell ill with a fever after crossing the Syr Darya (Seyhun). His doctors tried to treat him, including packing him in ice. He died at Otrar, the Chinese expedition was abandoned, and his body was taken to Semerkant. — [1911 Britannica](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Tim%C5%ABr); [History Today](https://www.historytoday.com/archive/months-past/death-tamerlane)
- **VERIFIED:** He was meant to be buried in Keş/Şehrisebz, but the passes were snowed in, so he was buried in Gür-i Emir in Semerkant. — [Gur-e Amir search extract, central-asia.guide / Britannica](https://central-asia.guide/uzbekistan/destinations-uz/samarkand/gur-e-amir/)
- **VERIFIED:** Timur named his grandson **Pir Muhammed** (son of Cihangir) as heir, but he was quickly sidelined. **Halil Sultan** (son of Miranşah) took Semerkant and Timur's treasury. This started the Timurid succession war (1405–1409). — [Wikipedia, Timurid War of Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_War_of_Succession); [Wikipedia, Pir Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pir_Muhammad_(son_of_Jahangir))
- **VERIFIED:** Halil Sultan defeated Pir Muhammed near Karşı in 1406. Pir Muhammed was murdered by his vizier Pir Ali Taz (1407; sources differ on the timing). — [Wikipedia, Pir Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pir_Muhammad_(son_of_Jahangir))
- **VERIFIED:** **Şahruh**, Timur's youngest (fourth) son and ruler of Herat, took Semerkant in 1409. Halil Sultan was given Rey and died there in 1411. — [Wikipedia, Timurid War of Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_War_of_Succession); [Wikipedia, Khalil Sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalil_Sultan)
- **DISPUTED:** The date of Halil Sultan's accession in Semerkant is given as either 18 February or 18 March 1405. — same sources as above

### Inferences
- Reasons for the China campaign: secondary literature usually cites the Ming insult (Timur being treated as a vassal and the imprisonment of envoys) and jihad against the "infidel" Ming. No source for this was retrieved in this session, so see Gaps.
- Cliffhanger: "Dünyayı fethetmeye çıkan adamı, Çin'e varmadan kış durdurdu."

### Gaps
- **The alcohol claim:** no reliable source was retrieved. Ibn Arabşah and some later writers link the illness to heavy drinking in the cold, but this could not be confirmed here. Label it **DISPUTED/unverified** and do not present it as fact.
- The reason for the China campaign, the Ming Yongle emperor's side, and the imprisoned envoy Fu An were not confirmed in this session (Iranica "Timur" and Manz were not reachable).
- The 1404 kurultay and grandsons' weddings: no source retrieved.

## 4. Gür-i Emir and the nephrite (jade) tombstone

### Takeaway
Gür-i Emir was begun in 1403 for Timur's beloved grandson and heir Muhammed Sultan. Timur was buried there because the snow kept him from reaching Şehrisebz. The dark green nephrite tombstone was made from two pieces Uluğ Bey brought back in 1425 after a campaign against Moghulistan. Nadir Şah took it to Iran in 1740 and later returned it. The stone is split in two; whether it cracked when taken or on the way back varies by source.

### Cited Findings
- **VERIFIED:** Construction began in 1403 after the sudden death of Muhammed Sultan, Timur's heir apparent and favourite grandson, for whom it was intended. Britannica says it was completed in 1404; other sources say Uluğ Bey finished the work. — [Britannica, Gūr-e Amīr](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Gur-e-Amir); [Lonely Planet](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/points-of-interest/gur-e-amir-mausoleum/1384275)
- **VERIFIED (single strong source line):** In 1425, during Uluğ Bey's campaign (the Ketmen-tepe battle against Moghulistan), two large pieces of nephrite came into his hands. Semerkant craftsmen joined them to make Timur's tombstone. One travel source says the stone came from a Chinese palace and had served as a Mongol throne: **LEGEND/DISPUTED**. — [Eurasia Travel, "Nephrite gravestone"](https://eurasia.travel/uzbekistan/legends/nephrite-gravestone/); [Advantour](https://www.advantour.com/us/uzbekistan/legends/amir-timur-gravestone.htm)
- **VERIFIED:** In 1740 Nadir Şah's troops took the jade tombstone (and, by some accounts, the mosque gate) to Iran. — [Eurasia Travel](https://eurasia.travel/uzbekistan/legends/nephrite-gravestone/); [Wikipedia, Gur-e-Amir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gur-e-Amir)
- **DISPUTED:** When the stone broke. The Gur-e Amir pages on Wikipedia say it broke in two when Nadir Şah took it; other accounts say it cracked at the joint on the way back. — [search summary of Wikipedia / Eurasia Travel / Advantour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gur-e-Amir)
- **LEGEND:** Nadir Şah returned the stone after bad luck: an earthquake, illness, or his son's or daughter's illness, depending on the version, or because his advisers urged it. — [Advantour](https://www.advantour.com/us/uzbekistan/legends/amir-timur-gravestone.htm); [Eurasia Travel](https://eurasia.travel/uzbekistan/legends/nephrite-gravestone/)
- **DISPUTED:** The tombstone inscription "When I rise from the dead, the world shall tremble." A visitor guide places it on the tombstone in Arabic. Other sources say a second inscription inside the coffin threatens an invader worse than Timur. Neither has been confirmed by a scholarly source. — [central-asia.guide, Gur-e Amir](https://central-asia.guide/uzbekistan/destinations-uz/samarkand/gur-e-amir/); [Wikipedia, Curse of Timur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curse_of_Timur)

### Inferences
- "Dünyanın en büyük yeşim bloğu" (the world's largest jade block) is a common claim, but no reliable source supported it in this session. Use "dev, koyu yeşil yeşim taşı" instead.
- The scholarly consensus is that the real tombstone inscription is a genealogy (tracing Timur's line to the Mongol ancestress Alan Goa, with an Alid/divine-light motif). This could not be sourced in this session, so check it before use.

### Gaps
- The exact size of the stone (often given as about 1.92 m × 0.37 m in the literature) and the claim that it is the "largest jade block" are not confirmed.
- The full text of the genealogy inscription (Alan Goa, Cengiz Han's line) was not retrieved.
- Who else is buried there: commonly listed as Şahruh, Uluğ Bey, Miranşah, Muhammed Sultan, and Timur's teacher Mir Seyyid Bereke, but this was not confirmed in this session.

## 5. The 1941 Soviet exhumation and the "curse"

### Takeaway
A Soviet team including anthropologist Mikhail Gerasimov opened Timur's tomb in Gür-i Emir in June 1941. The skeleton matched the chronicles: lame in the right leg (the "Aksak"/Lame nickname), a wounded right arm, and tall for his time (about 170–172 cm). Germany invaded the USSR on 22 June 1941, and the story of the "curse" grew from this coincidence. People close to Gerasimov say the curse inscription is a fabrication. Timur was reburied with Islamic rites in November–December 1942, shortly before the Soviet victory at Stalingrad, which gave the legend its second half.

### Cited Findings
- **VERIFIED (date range):** The tomb was opened in June 1941, but sources give 19, 20 or 22 June. Operation Barbarossa (the German invasion) began on 22 June 1941. — [Wikipedia, Curse of Timur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curse_of_Timur); [gw2ru](https://www.gw2ru.com/history/3073-timurs-curse-tomb-opening)
  - Recommendation: "Haziran 1941… ve birkaç gün sonra, 22 Haziran'da Almanya SSCB'ye saldırdı."
- **VERIFIED:** Gerasimov's examination showed Timur was "tall for his race and time, lame as recorded in his right leg, and with a wound to his right arm." The chronicles say battle wounds (arrows) left him lame in the right leg and stiff in the right arm. — [Silk Road Foundation, "Tamerlane"](http://silkroadfoundation.org/artl/timur.shtml); [CPA Media, "Timur's Legacy"](https://www.cpamedia.com/article.php?pg=features&aid=120615124737)
- **DISPUTED (exact figure):** Height is given as about 5'8" (about 172–173 cm), about 172 cm, or 1.70 m. — [Silk Road Foundation](http://silkroadfoundation.org/artl/timur.shtml); [Uzbekistan Wanderer](https://uzbekistanwanderer.com/timur-tamerlane-history/)
- **DISPUTED:** Red/reddish hair. One source says red hair was still attached to the skull; another describes reddish hair in the beard or mustache. These are popular sources, and Gerasimov's original report was not retrieved. — [CPA Media](https://www.cpamedia.com/article.php?pg=features&aid=120615124737); [uzbek-travel.com](https://uzbek-travel.com/about-uzbekistan/monuments/gur-e-amir-mausoleum/)
- **VERIFIED (single extract):** The Uluğ Bey remains were consistent with his recorded death by beheading. — [search extract, Uzbekistan Wanderer / Gur-e Amir pages](https://uzbekistanwanderer.com/timur-tamerlane-history/). Uluğ Bey's 1449 murder is well known, but confirmation from Gerasimov's own report was not obtained.
- **DISPUTED/LEGEND:** The curse inscription. The popular version says the tomb or coffin carried a warning that whoever opened the tomb would unleash an invader more terrible than Timur. Wikipedia: "people close to Gerasimov claim that this story is a fabrication." One summary says a 2003 Russian documentary traced the story to a book, not a carving. — [Wikipedia, Curse of Timur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curse_of_Timur)
- **VERIFIED as a reported story (his own account):** Cameraman/filmmaker **Malik Kayumov** was on the expedition. In his book he writes that before the opening, elders at a teahouse warned it would bring a terrible war, and the scientists dismissed them. — [Wikipedia, Malik Qayumov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malik_Qayumov); [Wikipedia, Curse of Timur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curse_of_Timur)
  - The later part of the story (Kayumov reached Marshal Zhukov / Stalin at the front and asked for the remains to be returned): **LEGEND/DISPUTED**. It was not confirmed in this session.
- **VERIFIED (month), LEGEND (causation):** In November 1942 Gerasimov oversaw Timur's reburial with full Islamic rites. Local legend credits the reburial with the victory at Stalingrad, which came about a month later. — [Wikipedia, Curse of Timur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curse_of_Timur); [Wikipedia, Mikhail Gerasimov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gerasimov_(archaeologist))
  - Note: some sources say December 1942 (often 20 December). Not confirmed; use "1942 sonu / Kasım 1942".

### Inferences
- For the episode's main twist: present the scene and the coincidence as fact, and the inscription and causation as "efsaneye göre". This is honest and keeps the tension.
- The "Mongoloid features" detail comes from Gerasimov's facial reconstruction. The Silk Road Foundation text calls him a "Mongoloid man", but this needs care because of the racial typology of the period.

### Gaps
- Gerasimov's original report and the full team (Tashkent scholars such as T. N. Kary-Niyazov, the writer Sadriddin Ayni and others) were not confirmed. The often-cited "fused kneecap" (the right patella fused to the femur) was not found in any source in this session.
- The detail of the kneecap or arm (fused elbow) should be checked before use.
- Şahruh's and Miranşah's remains (Miranşah's skull) were not confirmed.

## 6. Legacy, Uzbekistan's rehabilitation, cultural memory, and cliffhanger hooks

### Takeaway
After Timur came the Timurid renaissance: Şahruh's Herat and Uluğ Bey's Semerkant observatory (1420s, destroyed 1449, rediscovered 1908). Babür, who descended from Timur, founded the Mughal dynasty in India. In independent Uzbekistan, Timur replaced Marx: the Tashkent statue was put up in 1993–94 with the motto "Kuch – adolatda" (Güç adalettedir), and 1996 was declared the "Year of Amir Temur". In Europe he lives on through Marlowe's Tamburlaine.

### Cited Findings
- **VERIFIED:** The Uluğ Bey Observatory was built in Semerkant in the 1420s by the Timurid astronomer-ruler Uluğ Bey (dates vary: 1424–1429, or completed about 1429). It was destroyed in 1449 and rediscovered in 1908. — [Wikipedia, Ulugh Beg Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulugh_Beg_Observatory); [Linda Hall Library](https://www.lindahall.org/about/news/scientist-of-the-day/ulugh-beg/)
- **VERIFIED:** Uluğ Bey was Şahruh's son and Timur's grandson. Babür writes about Uluğ Bey's observatory in his memoirs (the Babürname), which is a direct link from the Timurid to the Mughal line. — [Wikipedia, Ulugh Beg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulugh_Beg)
- **VERIFIED:** History Today notes Uluğ Bey was the favourite grandson of "Tamburlaine", the conqueror Marlowe wrote about. — [History Today, "Ulugh-beg: Star-Gazer in Samarkand"](https://www.historytoday.com/archive/ulugh-beg-star-gazer-samarkand)
- **VERIFIED:** The Karl Marx statue in Tashkent (standing since 1968) was removed after independence and replaced by an equestrian statue of Amir Timur. The square is now Amir Timur Square. — [Alamy archive caption](https://www.alamy.com/1988-view-of-karl-marx-statue-in-revolution-garden-tashkent-in-1993-this-statue-was-removed-and-replaced-with-one-of-tamburlaine-the-uzbek-national-hero-following-the-independence-of-uzbekistan-after-the-collapse-of-the-soviet-union-the-square-in-which-it-stands-is-now-called-amir-timur-square-image467957799.html); [Oxford and Empire](https://oxfordandempire.site.ox.ac.uk/article/monuments-and-memorialisation-lessons-russia-and-central-asia)
- **DISPUTED (date):** 1993 (installation/renaming) versus an official unveiling on 31 August 1994, the third anniversary of independence. — [Visit Tashkent](https://visittashkent.uz/places/amir-timur-square/); [Wikipedia, Amir Timur Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amir_Timur_Square)
- **VERIFIED:** The statue carries the inscription "Strength is in justice" (Kuch – adolatda) in four languages. — [Visit Tashkent](https://visittashkent.uz/places/amir-timur-square/)
- **VERIFIED:** Karimov encouraged the cult of Timur and declared 1996 the "Year of Amir Temur" (the Amir Timur Museum opened in Tashkent). — [Wikipedia, Amir Timur Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amir_Timur_Museum); [Russia's Periphery (William & Mary)](https://russiasperiphery.pages.wm.edu/central-asia/uzbekistan/general/statue-of-tamerlane/)

### Inferences: cliffhanger and closing hooks for the 3-part series
- End of Episode 2 → opening of Episode 3: "Yıldırım Bayezid'i yenen adam artık 68 yaşında, gözleri görmüyor… ama hâlâ bir hedefi var: Çin."
- Mid-episode hook: "200 bin kişilik ordu kışın yola çıktı. Otrar'da durdu. Bir daha hiç kalkmadı."
- Climax: "1941 Haziranı. Semerkant. Bilim insanları tabutu açıyor… Üç gün sonra Hitler Sovyetler'e saldırıyor." Then: "Efsaneye göre…" / "Gerçekte ise…" (the cut between legend and fact).
- Closing: "Haritadan silinen imparatorluk bir yıldız kataloğu (Uluğ Bey), bir Tac Mahal (Babür'ün torunları) ve bir heykel bıraktı: 'Güç adalettedir.'" Note: the Taj Mahal link (Babür → Şah Cihan) is not sourced in these notes. It is well known, but should be checked before use.
- Series "loop" hook: "Mezarı açılınca savaş çıktı, geri gömülünce savaş döndü. Tesadüf mü?"

### Gaps
- Babür's founding of the Mughal Empire in 1526 (Battle of Panipat) and his descent from Timur through his father's line (Ömer Şeyh Mirza → Ebu Said → Miranşah's line) were not confirmed with a source in this session. Both are widely known; check them against Britannica or TDV before use.
- The date of Marlowe's *Tamburlaine the Great* (about 1587–88) was not confirmed with a source in this session.
- Herat under Şahruh and Gevher Şad (the Timurid renaissance) was not sourced.
- How Timur is remembered in Persian, Indian and Turkish memory (as the destroyer of Delhi in 1398, as the victor over Bayezid at Ankara in 1402) was not covered in this session.
- TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi ("Timur", "Gūr-i Mîr") and Encyclopaedia Iranica ("Timur") could not be fetched (access blocked). These are the recommended sources for the final check.
